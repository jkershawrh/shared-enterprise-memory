OBJECTIVE

Build the foundation for shared enterprise memory in which:

- Immutable-ledger preserves consequential historical evidence.
- Governance establishes the official operating position.
- Projections explain what the history means now.
- Agents retrieve fast, governed context from projections.
- Outcomes show what happened after a decision or action.
- Different human and system perspectives remain visible rather than being flattened into fabricated consensus.

The ledger is the evidence/control plane.

The projection service is the memory serving/data plane.

Agents must not use the ledger as their primary memory query database.

CORE PREMISE

People do not remember organizational events identically.

Enterprise memory must preserve the distinction between:

- Observation: what a source reports seeing
- Assertion: what a person or system claims
- Interpretation: what someone believes the evidence means
- Decision: an officially adopted operating position
- Dissent: an objection or alternative interpretation
- Action: something requested, authorized, executed, failed, or rolled back
- Outcome: what was subsequently observed
- Supersession: what replaced an earlier decision or policy
- Revocation: something that is no longer valid
- Dispute: a challenge to an assertion, interpretation, or decision

An approved decision is not objective truth. It is an authorized operating position with scope, evidence, effective dates, dissent, and expected outcomes.

Do not create a design that converts repeated summaries into “truth” merely because agents retrieve them frequently. Prevent institutional hallucination.

TARGET ARCHITECTURE

Use this separation:

Agents and enterprise systems
    -> submit candidate assertions, decisions, actions, and outcomes

Governance admission boundary
    -> validates schema, identity, authority, evidence, classification, and scope

Immutable ledger
    -> preserves admitted consequential evidence and history

Projection worker
    -> consumes committed events idempotently and builds current state

Shared-memory projection store
    -> supports fast current-state and historical queries

Agent context gateway
    -> returns identity-aware, purpose-limited context bundles

The ledger records consequential changes.

Projections calculate what those changes mean now.

The ledger history is immutable. Projection state is mutable, reproducible, and disposable.

PERFORMANCE AND INFRASTRUCTURE CONSTRAINTS

Infrastructure load is a primary design constraint.

The solution must ensure:

- Ordinary memory reads make zero ledger calls.
- Context assembly makes zero ledger calls.
- Agents do not call VerifyProof for every returned memory.
- Raw prompts, chain-of-thought, routine telemetry, and ordinary tool calls are not stored in the ledger.
- High-risk or irreversible operations may synchronously wait for a ledger receipt.
- Lower-risk observations and outcomes may use asynchronous durable delivery.
- Projection processing is at-least-once and idempotent.
- A projection can be rebuilt deterministically by replaying history.
- Projectors track offsets, freshness, schema version, failures, and checkpoints.
- Proof status is cached or periodically verified.
- Full verification is performed during audit, replay, checkpoint validation, or high-risk workflows.
- Hot entry_type chains are avoided through intentional domain, source, or tenant partitioning.
- Backpressure and lag are observable.
- The architecture can later support batch ingestion and signed checkpoints.

A projector may retrieve one complete ledger entry for each sparse consequential event if required by the current outbox contract. Do not place that retrieval on the agent read path. Introduce a source abstraction that can support batch retrieval or streaming envelopes later.

Do not add Kafka, AMQ Streams, a graph database, or a vector database merely to make the architecture appear enterprise-ready. Use interfaces for future infrastructure and implement the smallest coherent slice with existing repository patterns.

- Missing production-like evidence
- Missing scale or resilience validation
- Missing required review
- Implemented but not sufficiently evidenced

YELLOW is not production-ready.

GREEN

- Requirement is satisfied
- Automated evidence is reproducible
- Required controls passed
- Evidence is tied to the current commit and relevant environment
- No contradictory evidence remains
- Required review is complete

If evidence becomes stale, unverifiable, or materially mismatched with the deployed configuration, return the row to RED or YELLOW.

===============================================================================
21. PRODUCTION-READINESS RUBRIC
===============================================================================

Score every category from 0 through 4:

- 0: absent
- 1: designed but not demonstrated
- 2: demonstrated in development or fixtures
- 3: demonstrated in a production-like environment
- 4: reproducible, controlled, observed, documented, and operationally owned

Apply these weights:

- Correctness and deterministic projection: 15
- Contracts and compatibility: 10
- Security, identity, and authorization: 15
- Tenant isolation, privacy, and data governance: 10
- Resilience, replay, backup, and recovery: 10
- Performance, scale, and backpressure: 10
- Observability and operations: 10
- OpenShift and OpenShell integration: 10
- Supply-chain and evidence integrity: 5
- Documentation, usability, and runbooks: 5

Normalize the result to 100 points.

Production requires:

- At least 90/100
- No critical RED rows
- Every P0 and P1 requirement GREEN
- No security or tenant-isolation category below GREEN
- No expired waiver
- Two consecutive successful production-candidate executions
- Production-like OpenShift evidence
- Contract-compatibility evidence
- Representative load evidence
- Soak evidence
- Backup and restore evidence
- Deterministic replay evidence
- Upgrade and rollback evidence
- Security and abuse-test evidence
- Observability and alert validation
- Independent human review
- Explicit human promotion approval

A high numerical score cannot override a veto condition.

===============================================================================
22. AUTOMATIC NO-GO CONDITIONS
===============================================================================

The result is automatically NO-GO if any of the following are true:

- A normal context read requires a ledger call.
- Cross-tenant data exposure is possible.
- Authorization fails open.
- Unverified input is presented as trusted evidence.
- A checksum is represented as signer-bound verification.
- Duplicate delivery changes effective state.
- Replay produces a different projection.
- Rebuild loses dissent, dispute, supersession, revocation, or provenance.
- Routine OpenShell or OCSF telemetry floods the ledger.
- Fixture evidence is labeled as live evidence.
- Consequential actions lack actor or correlation provenance.
- Required secrets appear in source, images, manifests, or logs.
- Backup restoration has not been demonstrated.
- Rollback has not been demonstrated.
- Critical vulnerabilities remain without an approved, time-bounded exception.
- A context bundle leaks unauthorized record existence.
- Stale projection state is silently presented as current.
- Observability cannot distinguish ledger, projection, policy, and dependency failures.
- A required GREEN result cannot be reproduced.
- Production qualification relies only on self-attestation.

24. WAIVERS
===============================================================================

A waiver does not convert RED into GREEN.

Every waiver must contain:

- Exact failed or missing requirement
- Business justification
- Risk severity
- Named accountable owner
- Compensating control
- Scope
- Creation date
- Expiration date
- Remediation issue
- Explicit approval

The following are not waivable for production:

- Cross-tenant exposure
- Authorization fail-open
- Evidence-integrity misrepresentation
- Non-deterministic replay
- Unrecoverable projections
- Missing backup restoration
- Critical secret exposure
- Unbounded routine telemetry ingestion

Avoid unsupported claims such as:

- tamper-proof

- Red Hat certified
- OpenShell validated
- cryptographically signed

unless the evidence and implementation justify those exact statements.

Explain projections in plain language:

A projection is a rebuildable, read-optimized interpretation of immutable events. It lets agents retrieve current, relevant organizational context without repeatedly scanning or calling the immutable ledger. Different projections can serve different purposes while still pointing back to the same evidence.
